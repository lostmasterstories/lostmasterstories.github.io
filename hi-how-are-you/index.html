Hi, How Are You

The story behind Daniel Johnston's Hi, How Are You: The Unfinished Album (1983), the homemade cassette recorded on a $59 Sanyo machine and handed out for free in Austin, Texas. How the frog on its cover, Jeremiah the Innocent, ended up on Kurt Cobain's T-shirt at the 1992 MTV Video Music Awards, and what happened next: MTV's The Cutting Edge, a bidding war, Atlantic Records and the Austin mural that outlived its building. A music history documentary with a source for every fact.

Kurt Cobain's Hi, How Are You shirt 0:00

September 9, 1992. The MTV Video Music Awards. Kurt Cobain walks out in front of millions of viewers wearing a homemade-looking T-shirt. On it, a strange little frog with eyes on stalks, and three words: Hi, how are you. That frog came from a cassette tape. A tape recorded nine years earlier, on a fifty-nine dollar Sanyo machine, by a young man almost nobody had heard of. His name was Daniel Johnston. And the tape was called Hi, How Are You. So how does a homemade cassette, recorded alone and handed out for free, end up on the chest of the biggest rock star in the world? This is that story.

Daniel Johnston's basement tapes 0:40

Daniel Dale Johnston was born on January 22, 1961, in Sacramento, California. He was the youngest of five children in a fundamentalist Christian family. His father had been a fighter pilot in the Second World War. The family later moved east, to the small town of New Cumberland, West Virginia. In the late 1970s, Daniel started writing songs on the family piano and on a chord organ. And he started recording them, in the basement, on cheap cassette tapes. His first album, Songs of Pain, came out in 1981. He recorded the whole thing on a home cassette recorder. No studio, no engineer. Just Daniel, a keyboard and a tape running. His biggest influence, he always said, was the Beatles. He even compared himself to them. A kid in a West Virginia basement, measuring himself against the biggest band in history. He went on to study art at a branch of Kent State University. He drew constantly. Characters, eyeballs, ghosts, and a frog that would one day become very famous.

Recording Hi, How Are You on a $59 Sanyo (1983) 1:48

In 1983, Daniel left home and went to live with his brother, Dick, in Houston, Texas. There, in a single year, he recorded two of the albums he is now best known for. One was Yip/Jump Music. The other was Hi, How Are You. There was no studio. No producer. No band. Just a fifty-nine dollar Sanyo cassette recorder, a voice, and whatever instrument was at hand. The tape had fifteen tracks. He called it Hi, How Are You: The Unfinished Album. And on the cardboard insert, he drew the frog. Side A opens with a song called Poor You. Further in, there's Walking the Cow, and I Picture Myself With a Guitar. Short, raw songs, some of them barely finished, sung straight into the microphone. It was self-released. Which, in Daniel's case, meant something very simple. He made the copies himself.

Handing out cassettes in Austin 2:40

After Houston, Daniel spent five months working with a traveling carnival. Then he settled in Austin, Texas. He took a job at a McDonald's on the street everyone in Austin calls The Drag, right next to the university. And he did something that would define his career. He handed out his tapes. To people he met. To people on the street. To anyone who would take one. His tapes even had their own label. He called it Stress Records. It was a label of one, and its catalogue was whatever Daniel had recorded that month. A writer at the Austin Chronicle later remembered a skinny kid showing up at the office door, handing him a tape, and saying: I'm Daniel Johnston, here's a tape of mine for you to listen to.

MTV's The Cutting Edge and the McDonald's phone 3:24

In 1985, MTV came to Austin to film an episode of The Cutting Edge, a show about new music scenes. Plenty of people told the crew they had to include Daniel. They did. When it aired, the shy kid with the cheap tapes was the unexpected star. And suddenly, the phone started ringing. At McDonald's. Journalists and record label executives called the restaurant, because the McDonald's phone was the only way to reach him. Other musicians started covering his songs. The Dead Milkmen recorded his Rocket Ship. And in 1988, Homestead Records put Hi, How Are You out on vinyl. Quick pause. Mental illness and the 1990 plane incident 4:15

But there is another side to this story, and it matters. Daniel Johnston lived with schizophrenia and bipolar disorder. In 1990, during a manic episode, he was flying in a small plane piloted by his father. Daniel pulled out the ignition key. His father had to make a forced landing. They both survived with minor injuries. Daniel was committed to a psychiatric hospital. And it was from there, far from the stage, that his music was about to reach further than ever before.

Jeremiah the Innocent and the 1992 VMAs 4:47

Back to September 1992. Nirvana's Nevermind had come out almost exactly a year earlier, and Kurt Cobain was one of the most watched musicians on the planet. At the MTV Video Music Awards, he wore the shirt. The frog from Hi, How Are You. A drawing that had started on a cardboard cassette insert. The frog even had a name. Jeremiah the Innocent. And Cobain wasn't just wearing a cool shirt. He was a real fan. He was especially attached to Yip/Jump Music, the other album Daniel had recorded in Houston, and he listed it among his favorite records. Millions of people who had never heard a Daniel Johnston song now wanted to know who he was. And the record industry wanted to know too.

Elektra, Metallica and Atlantic Records 5:32

Major labels started competing to sign him. One of them was Elektra. Daniel turned it down. The reason? Elektra released records by Metallica. And Daniel believed Metallica were possessed by the devil. In February 1994 he signed with Atlantic instead. His album Fun came out that year, produced by Paul Leary of the Butthole Surfers. It was not a success. By 1996, Atlantic had dropped him. The major label chapter lasted about two years. The homemade tapes have lasted more than forty.

The Austin mural and Hi, How Are You Day 6:07

In 1993, a record store on The Drag called Sound Exchange asked Daniel to paint the frog on the side of its building, at 21st and Guadalupe. The store closed in 2003. Restaurants came and went. In 2023 the building itself was demolished. The wall with the frog was left standing. Tom Waits covered his songs. So did Yo La Tengo. In 2004 a whole album of covers came out, with Beck and the Flaming Lips among the artists. And they kept coming back to him. In 2019, at Austin's Hi, How Are You Day, the Flaming Lips and Yo La Tengo were on stage playing his songs. He never stopped drawing, either. In 2012 he released a comic book called Space Ducks, with an album to go with it. In 2005, the documentary The Devil and Daniel Johnston won the directing award for documentaries at the Sundance Film Festival. In the fall of 2017 he played a short farewell tour of five dates. He died in September 2019, at fifty-eight, at his home near Houston. Today, Austin celebrates his birthday, January 22, as Hi, How Are You Day. And a project in his name uses his story to start open conversations about mental health. So how did a homemade cassette end up on the chest of the biggest rock star in the world? Because Daniel gave it away. One tape, one person, one hello at a time. Until somebody wore it on television. If this story stayed with you, subscribe to Lost Master Stories for the next one. Hi.
